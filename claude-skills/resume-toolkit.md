# Resume Toolkit

Three jobs, one skill. Run them in this order:

| Stage | Question it answers | Section |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Tailor | WHICH of my true experiences belong on this resume, in what order? | Part 1 |
| 2. Write | HOW is each line worded so it reads as achievement, not duty? | Part 2 |
| 3. ATS | WILL it parse, and does it hit the JD's keywords? | Part 3 |

Companion skills: `resume-format` (Word/.docx layout contract) and `cover-letter` (the letter half
of the same application). This skill decides content; `resume-format` decides rendering.

## When to Use This Skill

Any of: customize/tailor a resume for a specific posting, create a targeted version, match a job
description, reorder or reframe experience for a target role, write or improve bullet points,
transform weak descriptions into achievements, add metrics and quantifiable results, check ATS
compatibility, run keyword-match analysis, or diagnose why applications aren't getting responses.

Also use unprompted when you see weak bullets (passive language, no metrics, vague duties), or when
the user supplies a resume file and mentions they're applying to jobs.

**Combined capabilities:** reorder sections by relevance · rewrite the summary for a specific
position · add JD keywords truthfully · apply STAR / CAR / X-Y-Z to every bullet · surface
quantifiable metrics · test ATS parsing and formatting · calculate and re-score keyword match ·
produce multiple targeted versions · keep every claim authentic.

## Part 1 — Tailoring (strategy)

### The Tailoring Philosophy

**Key Principle:** You're not lying or fabricating — you're HIGHLIGHTING the most relevant parts of
your true experience.

Think of your full experience as a library of achievements. Tailoring means selecting the books that
best fit what each employer is looking for.

### Tailoring Process

**Step 1: Analyze the Job**
- Identify required skills and keywords
- Note the company's priorities
- Understand the role's primary responsibilities

**Step 2: Audit Your Resume.** For each section, ask:
- Does this support my candidacy for THIS specific role?
- Is there a better way to phrase this for THIS job?
- Should this be higher or lower in priority?

**Step 3: Make Strategic Adjustments**
- **Professional Summary:** rewrite to mirror the job's key requirements
- **Skills Section:** reorder most-relevant-first, add missing keywords
- **Experience:** reorder jobs if a less recent role is more relevant; swap bullets to lead with the
  most relevant achievement; add keywords naturally into existing bullets
- **Education:** highlight relevant coursework and certifications

### Section-by-Section Tailoring Guide

#### Professional Summary

Your "elevator pitch" — customize for each application.

**Generic (AVOID):**
```
Results-driven professional with 5 years of experience in business operations. Strong analytical and communication skills. Looking for a challenging opportunity to grow.
```

**Tailored for Operations Manager:**
```
Operations Manager with 5 years optimizing supply chain processes and reducing costs by 25%. Expertise in Lean Six Sigma, vendor management, and cross-functional team leadership. Track record of improving operational efficiency while maintaining quality standards.
```

**Tailored for Project Manager (same person):**
```
Project Manager with 5 years leading cross-functional initiatives from concept to delivery. PMP-certified with expertise in Agile methodology, stakeholder management, and budget oversight. Track record of on-time, under-budget project delivery across $10M+ portfolios.
```

#### Skills Section Reordering

**JD emphasizes:** Data analysis, SQL, Python, stakeholder communication

**Before:** Microsoft Office, Communication, Project Management, Python, SQL, Data Visualization, Leadership
**After:** SQL, Python, Data Analysis, Data Visualization, Stakeholder Communication, Project Management, Microsoft Office

#### Experience Section

**Strategy 1: Reorder jobs.** If your most recent job is less relevant, move the relevant one up
(dates stay honest and visible). Applying for a data role: lead with Data Analyst, keep Marketing
Coordinator below it.

**Strategy 2: Swap bullet order.** Lead with what the role cares about.
- Management role → "Led team of 12…", "Managed budget of $2M…"
- Technical role → "Developed automated system…", "Analyzed 500K+ data points…"

**Strategy 3: Adjust bullet language.** Incorporate JD keywords while staying truthful.
- JD says: "stakeholder management"
- Your bullet: "Worked with various teams"
- Tailored: "Managed stakeholder relationships across 5 departments, ensuring alignment on project priorities"

### Truth vs. Tailoring Line

**Acceptable tailoring:** reordering true information · emphasizing relevant experience · using
industry-standard terminology · adding context to vague statements · matching the JD's language style.

**Unacceptable (lying):** adding skills you don't have · changing numbers or metrics · creating fake
experiences · claiming titles you didn't hold · stating certifications you don't have.

### Version Management

Maintain ONE master resume containing every experience and every bullet ever written — the source of
truth. Generate targeted versions from it, and save the tailoring notes for interview prep.

```
[LastName]_Resume_[TargetRole]_[Company]_[Date].pdf

Smith_Resume_PM_Google_Jan2024.pdf
Smith_Resume_DataAnalyst_Meta_Jan2024.pdf
Smith_Resume_General_Master.docx
```

## Part 2 — Bullet Writing (craft)

### The Bullet Point Problem

Most resumes list duties instead of achievements. Every bullet must be active, specific, and
quantified. Never open with "Responsible for", "Helped with", "Assisted in", or "Participated in" —
replace with a power verb and attach a number.

### Core Frameworks

#### 1. The X-Y-Z Formula (Google Method)

**"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"**

```
❌ BEFORE: "Managed social media accounts"
✅ AFTER: "Grew Instagram following by 250% (5K to 17.5K) by implementing daily content calendar and influencer partnerships"

X = Grew Instagram following by 250%
Y = 5K to 17.5K followers
Z = Daily content calendar + influencer partnerships
```

```
❌ BEFORE: "Improved customer service"
✅ AFTER: "Increased customer satisfaction scores by 40% (3.2 to 4.5/5) by redesigning support ticketing system and training 15 agents"
```

#### 2. STAR Method

**S**ituation → **T**ask → **A**ction → **R**esult.

Full story: "Inherited underperforming sales team (S) with 65% quota attainment. Tasked with
improving performance within Q1 (T). Implemented new training program and revised commission
structure (A). Achieved 92% quota attainment by Q2, generating $1.8M additional revenue (R)."

Condensed for the resume: "Revitalized underperforming sales team through training program and
commission restructure, improving quota attainment from 65% to 92% and generating $1.8M additional
revenue"

#### 3. CAR Method

**C**hallenge → **A**ction → **R**esult.

"Reduced customer churn (C) by implementing proactive outreach program (A), retaining 85% of at-risk
accounts worth $500K ARR (R)"

### Power Verbs by Category

**Leadership & Management:** Led, Directed, Managed, Supervised, Coordinated, Spearheaded,
Orchestrated, Oversaw, Championed, Mentored

**Achievement & Success:** Achieved, Delivered, Exceeded, Surpassed, Attained, Secured, Won, Earned,
Captured, Clinched

**Growth & Improvement:** Grew, Increased, Boosted, Expanded, Scaled, Elevated, Amplified, Maximized,
Accelerated, Doubled

**Creation & Innovation:** Created, Developed, Designed, Built, Launched, Pioneered, Established,
Founded, Introduced, Originated

**Optimization & Efficiency:** Streamlined, Optimized, Enhanced, Improved, Revitalized, Transformed,
Restructured, Modernized, Automated, Simplified

**Analysis & Strategy:** Analyzed, Assessed, Evaluated, Identified, Diagnosed, Researched,
Investigated, Examined, Audited, Forecasted

**Collaboration & Communication:** Collaborated, Partnered, Facilitated, Coordinated, United,
Presented, Communicated, Negotiated, Influenced, Persuaded

**Problem-Solving:** Resolved, Solved, Troubleshot, Rectified, Debugged, Eliminated, Reduced,
Mitigated, Prevented, Corrected

### Quantification Strategies

Every bullet should carry at least ONE number.

**1. Money** — revenue generated ("$2M in new revenue"), money saved ("Reduced costs by $50K
annually"), budget managed ("Managed $5M project budget"), deal size ("Closed 3 enterprise deals
worth $500K")

**2. Percentages** — growth ("Increased conversion rate by 45%"), improvement ("Reduced error rate by
60%"), efficiency ("Decreased processing time by 30%"), quality ("Improved accuracy from 85% to 98%")

**3. Time** — speed ("Reduced load time from 8s to 2s"), frequency ("Delivered weekly reports to 50+
stakeholders"), duration ("Completed 6-month project in 4 months"), time saved ("Automated process,
saving team 10 hours/week")

**4. Scale/Volume** — people ("Led team of 15", "Trained 50+ employees"), projects ("Managed 8
concurrent initiatives"), customers ("Served 500+ enterprise clients"), users ("Built product used by
100K+ daily active users")

**5. Comparison/Before-After** — "Increased from X to Y" · "Reduced from X to Y" · "Grew from X to Y"

#### When You Don't Have Exact Numbers

- **Approximate with ~** — "Improved performance by ~40%"
- **Use ranges** — "Managed team of 8-12 people", "Generated $50K-$75K in monthly revenue"
- **Estimate conservatively** — if you think it was 60%, say 50%; if 100 hours, say 75
- **Quantify inputs when outputs are unknown** — "Conducted 30+ customer interviews", "Analyzed 500+
  data points", "Created 20+ marketing campaigns"
- **Find related metrics** — can't measure conversion? measure traffic, engagement, or another funnel stage

### Bullet Writing Process

1. **Start with the weak bullet** — "Managed social media"
2. **Ask clarifying questions** — What platforms? How many followers? What growth? What specific
   actions? What business impact?
3. **Gather information** — Instagram + LinkedIn; 5K → 18K; daily content calendar + influencer
   partnerships; 200 leads generated
4. **Apply the formula** — "Grew Instagram and LinkedIn following by 260% (5K to 18K) by implementing
   daily content calendar and 15 influencer partnerships, generating 200+ qualified leads"
5. **Optimize length** — prioritize the most impressive metrics: "Grew social media audience by 260%
   (5K to 18K followers) through content strategy and influencer partnerships, generating 200+ leads"

### Bullet Strength Checklist

Every bullet must have:
- ✅ Strong action verb (never "responsible for", "helped", "assisted")
- ✅ At least one number/metric
- ✅ Specific outcome or result, not a duty
- ✅ Context of scale (team size, budget, users, volume)
- ✅ **1–2 lines maximum** — if it runs longer, cut to the strongest metric
- ✅ Reads naturally when spoken aloud
- ✅ Relevant to the target role

## Part 3 — ATS Optimization (compliance)

### The ATS Problem

75% of resumes are rejected by Applicant Tracking Systems before a human sees them. ATS filter
unqualified candidates automatically, search for keywords from the requirements, parse resumes into
structured data, and rank by keyword-match percentage.

Resumes fail because of: poor formatting (tables, columns, headers/footers) · missing JD keywords ·
inconsistent section headers · non-standard fonts or special characters · text embedded in images ·
wrong file format.

### ATS Compatibility Checklist

**File format**
- ✅ .docx or .pdf (not .pages, .odt); PDFs must be text-based, not scanned
- ✅ File name: "FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf"

**Font & formatting**
- ✅ Standard fonts: Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Times New Roman
- ✅ 10–12pt body, 14–16pt headers
- ✅ No text boxes, tables, or columns · no headers/footers · no images, graphics, or charts
- ✅ Consistent date format (MM/YYYY) · standard bullets (•, -, *)

**Section headers** — use standard names ATS recognize: "Professional Experience" or "Work
Experience", "Education", "Skills", "Summary" or "Professional Summary".

**Contact information** — in the body, never the header/footer:
```
John Smith
email@example.com | (555) 123-4567 | LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/johnsmith
San Francisco, CA
```
Avoid tables for contact info, special characters in the email, multiple phone numbers, or a full
mailing address (city/state is enough).

### Keyword Optimization Process

#### Step 1: Extract JD keywords — three types

- **Hard skills:** languages (Python, Java, SQL), tools (Salesforce, AWS, Excel), certifications
  (PMP, CPA, CFA), methodologies (Agile, Six Sigma, SDLC)
- **Soft skills:** leadership, collaboration, communication, problem-solving, analytical thinking,
  project management, stakeholder management
- **Industry terms:** B2B, SaaS, e-commerce, enterprise, SMB, mid-market, ARR, MRR, churn rate

#### Step 2: Match analysis

For each keyword: does the exact phrase appear? are there synonyms/variations? how often? where
(summary, experience, skills)?

Watch for near-misses — "risk mitigation" when the JD says "risk management" is close but not an
exact match. Watch for the reverse failure too: JD requires "Python, SQL, Data Visualization" and the
resume says "programming, databases, making charts."

#### Step 3: Calculate match score

```
Match Score = (Keywords Matched / Total Required Keywords) × 100

20 required keywords, 15 present = 75%
Target: 80%+ for a strong match
```

#### Step 4: Placement strategy

- **Priority 1 — Professional Summary:** 5–8 most important keywords, natural, 3–4 sentences.
  "Data Scientist with 5+ years using Python, SQL, and machine learning to drive business insights…"
- **Priority 2 — Skills Section:** list keywords explicitly, grouped by category, using the JD's
  exact phrasing
- **Priority 3 — Experience Bullets:** work keywords into achievement statements; never force them

**Density:** critical keywords 2–4× across the resume; important keywords 1–2×; vary phrasing ("led
team" / "team leadership"); never stack near-duplicates ("Python, Python programming, Python
developer, Python expert").

#### Keyword Integration Rules

**DO:** add keywords that truthfully describe your work · use exact JD phrasing when accurate · place
keywords naturally in context · repeat across summary, skills, and experience.

**DON'T:** add skills you don't have · keyword stuff · create a meaning different from your actual
experience · sacrifice readability for density.

### Industry-Specific Considerations

- **Tech:** emphasize languages and frameworks; put GitHub and portfolio links in Skills (not the
  header); certifications and courses matter
- **Business/Finance:** software proficiency (Excel, SAP, Salesforce); certifications critical (CPA,
  CFA, PMP); industry keywords (P&L, ROI, KPI)
- **Healthcare:** licenses and certifications required; named systems (Epic, Cerner, MEDITECH);
  compliance keywords (HIPAA, Joint Commission)
- **Marketing:** platform expertise (HubSpot, Salesforce, Google Analytics); channel keywords (SEO,
  PPC, email marketing); metrics-driven language

## Special Cases

**Entry-level / recent graduates.** Education becomes a keyword-carrying section — include relevant
coursework and projects. Draw achievements from academic projects with real impact, internships
(these count as experience; use their keywords), and club leadership with metrics. Example: "Led
university marketing club of 50 members, organizing 8 events attended by 300+ students and securing
$10K in corporate sponsorships"

**Career changers.** Lead with transferable skills and reframe old experience for the new industry.
Use keywords from the TARGET industry, not the current one; you may need two ATS versions. "Managed
cross-functional teams of 15 (retail)" becomes "Led cross-functional teams of 15 across operations,
merchandising, and customer experience, coordinating workflows and achieving 100% project completion
rate."

**Employment gaps.** Don't draw attention — fill the space with freelance/consulting work, volunteer
achievements, side projects, and professional development, all keyword-bearing. Years-only dates
(instead of months) can help.

**Executive level.** ATS still applies. Focus on strategic keywords, board experience, P&L size, and
team size.

## Final Checklist

Before submitting any resume:

1. ✅ Summary names the exact job title/function and carries 5–8 top keywords
2. ✅ Top 5 skills match the JD's top 5 requirements
3. ✅ Most relevant experience positioned first; each role's top bullet answers a key requirement
4. ✅ Every bullet passes the Bullet Strength Checklist (power verb, metric, result, ≤2 lines)
5. ✅ Keywords appear naturally across summary, skills, and experience — critical ones 2–4×
6. ✅ Company and industry terminology used correctly
7. ✅ All claims truthful and traceable to the master resume
8. ✅ ATS parsing clean: standard headers, no tables/columns/graphics, contact info in body
9. ✅ Match score re-calculated after changes — 80%+ for a strong match
10. ✅ File named `[LastName]_Resume_[Role]_[Company]_[Date]` and saved with tailoring notes for
    interview prep

**Implementation notes:** always analyze the JD before tailoring · keep tailoring changes documented
for interview prep · the master resume stays the single source of truth · never sacrifice ATS
compatibility for tailoring · re-test keyword match after every round of edits.
