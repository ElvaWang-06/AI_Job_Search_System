# Cover Letter 

This skill governs cover letters. Content selection follows the same truthfulness rule as the resume skills; the Output Formatting Contract in the final section is non-negotiable and applies to the delivered letter, not to this document.

## When to use this skill

Use when the user wants to:

- Write a cover letter for a specific job or company
- Tailor an existing cover letter to a new job description
- Turn resume/experience material into a letter for an application

Use alongside resume-tailor when a full application (resume + letter) is being prepared for the same role, so both documents emphasize the same JD keywords and the same true experience.

## Content sourcing (inherited, non-negotiable)
The master resume / experience material is the only source of truth. Select, reframe, and emphasize — but never introduce an experience, responsibility, achievement, or metric that is not explicitly in the source material. A cover letter may connect and narrate real facts, but it may not invent them. Any wording that upgrades the strength of a claim ("supported" → "led") must be confirmable against the source.

## Tailoring to the job
- Read the JD first. Identify the role's primary function, the top 2–3 requirements, and the language the employer uses, then mirror that language where it is truthful.
- Lead with the strongest, most relevant point of alignment — not a generic opening.
- Show fit through specific, real evidence (a project, an outcome, a metric already in the source material), not adjectives.
- Reference the company or role concretely enough that the letter could not be pasted, unchanged, into a different application.

## Structure and length
Aim for three to four short paragraphs, roughly 250–400 words, on a single page:

- An opening that states the role and the core reason the user is a strong fit.
- One or two middle paragraphs of concrete evidence tied to the JD's priorities.
- A closing that signals genuine interest and a straightforward call to action.

The letter opens with a greeting to the hiring team (or a named person if known) and ends with a simple sign-off followed by Elva Wang's name. These are ordinary prose lines, not headings.

## Tone

Warm, direct, and professional. Confident without overclaiming. Specific over effusive. No filler openers ("I am writing to apply for..."), no clichés, no restating the resume line by line — the letter adds narrative and motivation the resume can't carry.

## Output Formatting Contract (hard constraints — the finished letter must follow all of these)
- Output plain text only.
- Do not use horizontal lines, separators, divider lines, or dashed lines (such as ---, ***, ___, or any similar formatting).
- Do not use Markdown formatting, tables, bullet points, or numbered lists in the cover letter.
- Present the cover letter as continuous prose with standard paragraph breaks only.
- Do not include headings such as "Introduction," "Conclusion," or paragraph labels.
- Do not include placeholders or template instructions in the final output.
- The final deliverable should be ready to copy directly into a Word document or an online application without any formatting cleanup.

If any required detail is genuinely missing (e.g. the hiring manager's name), ask for it or omit it gracefully — never leave a bracketed placeholder in the delivered letter.
